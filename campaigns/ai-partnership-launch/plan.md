## 0. Studio constraints applied

- **Ask before deleting.** Nothing here deletes list data or files.

**What the business is (from `brand/voice.md`, imported by `AGENTS.md`).** We partner with marketing agencies that already serve clients. We provide the AI capability they do not have; the agency keeps the client relationship. The path is build, hand over, support. We sign off as a real person on the partnerships team and name one next step.

**Segment choice (decided).** `brand/voice.md` targets agencies that already serve clients but do not offer AI services. The third group stays inside that: agencies with a client asking for AI now. Agencies that already sell AI services are left out, because they contradict the current positioning and would need a different offer.

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** AI partnership launch
**Summary:** Open our AI partnership to the 2,000-agency waitlist over four weeks of email, with a different message for each of the three agency groups, and a scoping call as the single next step.
**Primary objective:** 300 waitlist agencies book a scoping call by 3 January 2027.
**Secondary objectives:**
- Track signed partnerships on a longer window, so the four-week call goal connects to revenue.
- Learn which of the three groups responds, so the next campaign targets the best one.
- Produce reusable copy for the first month of partner onboarding.

**Reality check, stated plainly.** 300 calls from 2,000 is 15%. That is a high bar for four weeks of email in December, but this is a call goal, not a closed-deal goal: the call is a 30-minute conversation, not a signed contract. Treat 300 as a target, not a forecast, and watch the show rate.

**Measurement split (decided):**
- 4-week goal: 300 scoping calls booked.
- After the four weeks: track closed partnerships on a longer window.
- Do not use signed partnerships as the four-week target; the sale needs a call, a scope, and a start date.

**Segment sizes are unknown.** "About 2,000" across three groups, no split. Pull the real counts before setting per-group targets.

## 2. Target Audience

All three groups joined the waitlist, so they already raised a hand. That is the advantage: this is warm email, not cold outreach.

### 2.1 Agencies with no AI background
They serve clients well and have no AI capability in house. Clients are starting to ask for AI work, and the agency either declines it or quietly outsources it without a system. They fear hiring, tool sprawl, and selling something they cannot deliver. They need a plain explanation of what we do, who does the work, and what a first project looks like.
Buying stage: consideration. Message job: make the partnership easy to understand and low-risk.

### 2.2 Agencies with partial AI background
They have tried tools or run one or two AI projects, but delivery is not repeatable. One person knows the tools; nobody owns the process. They need to turn experiments into client work they can scope and repeat, and to fill the gaps they cannot staff.
Buying stage: consideration to decision. Message job: show the step from experiments to dependable delivery.

### 2.3 Agencies with a client asking for AI now
They serve clients and do not sell AI services, but a client has asked for AI work, or will in the next quarter. Right now they either say no or quietly hand the work to a freelancer without a process. They need a partner who can take that work behind their brand and stay accountable for it. This group has a live client request, so it is the first segment to test. Confirm its size before setting a target.
Buying stage: decision. Message job: "You have the client. We have the capability." Make the first step a scoping call about that specific request.

**Where they spend time:** their inbox, then a call. Email is the only channel this campaign uses, so the work is subject lines, segment fit, and one clear next step.

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message:** We build and run the AI capability your clients are asking for. You keep the client relationship. Start with one scoping call.

Message hierarchy: why care, what it is, why us, what to do next.

**Supporting messages (confirm the offer facts before use):**

1. **For agencies with no AI background:** "You do not need to hire an AI team to sell AI work. We scope it, build it, and support it. You stay the client's agency." Why it matters: adds capability without adding headcount or a vendor the client sees.
2. **For agencies with partial AI background:** "You have tried the tools. We turn that into repeatable client delivery: a defined scope, a build, a handover, and support after." Why it matters: closes the gap between a demo and work the agency can sell again.
3. **For agencies with a client asking for AI now:** "You already have the client asking. We scope and build the AI work behind your brand, and you keep the relationship." Why it matters: turns a request they would decline into work they can accept.

**Proof points:** none are on file. Do not invent case studies, percentages, or client names. Use only what we will do (build, hand over, support) and real timelines. If the owner has approved references later, add them with permission.

**Words to avoid:** best-in-class, AI-powered, game-changer, instant, and any word that promises more than the sentence can show. No guaranteed results. No invented revenue numbers. No "we replace your team" — we add capability; they keep the relationship.

**What the AI does, in plain terms:** [one or two sentences the owner confirms — e.g. the specific work we build and hand over]. This must be written before Send 1, because the brand voice asks for the work itself, not a label.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

1. **300 calls in four weeks is a high bar for B2B services in December.** The waitlist is warm, but the sale needs a call and a scope.
   Mitigation: track calls booked as the four-week goal, watch the show rate, track closed deals on a longer window, and use the 20 December checkpoint.
2. **December is a holiday month for agency owners.** Replies and call attendance drop in the last two weeks.
   Mitigation: send the core offer messages in weeks 1 and 2, keep December sends short, and treat January as the real decision window.
3. **The list may not record who has a client asking for AI now.** Without that tag, the client-demand group cannot be mailed separately.
   Mitigation: add one question to the booking form or use an existing tag before week 2. If neither is possible, fold them into the no-AI message and run two segments instead of three.
4. **No offer facts on file.** Copy would fill gaps with guesses.
   Mitigation: confirm what we build, scope, timeline, price, and support before any draft is final. Bracketed placeholders stay until then.
5. **List age and deliverability.** A waitlist that has not been mailed may land in spam.
   Mitigation: authenticate the domain, send from one address, include plain text, keep unsubscribe one click.
6. **The studio rule against sending to real people.** An automated send would break it.
   Mitigation: the agent drafts only; a named person approves and sends; tests go to internal addresses.
