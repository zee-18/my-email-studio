## 0. Studio constraints applied

**Brand context gap (important).** `brand/voice.md` is written for marketing agency owners being pitched an AI partnership, not for a tea subscription. ## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** The Steep Club opens
**Summary:** Open The Steep Club to the 2,000-person waitlist over four weeks of email, with a different angle for each of the three waitlist groups.
**Primary objective:** 300 waitlist members start a paid subscription by 3 January 2027.
**Secondary objectives:**
- Keep unsubscribe rate low by sending to the right group with the right angle.
- Learn which group converts best, so the next campaign can repeat it.
- Produce reusable copy for the first month of subscription onboarding.

**The math, stated plainly:** 300 of 2,000 is 15%. That is a high bar for email alone, on a list that has been waiting. It is a target, not a forecast. Section 7 sets checkpoints so we find out early if it is out of reach.

**Segment sizes are unknown.** The brief says "about 2,000" across three groups but gives no split. Pull the real counts before setting per-group targets.

## 2. Target Audience

All three groups joined the same waitlist, so they have already shown interest. That is the advantage: this is not cold email.

### 2.1 Tea-curious beginners
People who like the idea of loose-leaf tea but do not know what to buy. They fear wasting money on tea they will not like, and they do not know how to brew it. They need a low-risk first box and simple brewing notes.
Buying stage: consideration, close to decision.

### 2.2 Gift buyers
People shopping for someone else in December. They care about the gift looking considered, arriving on time, and being easy for the recipient to use. They need a clear order-by date and a simple gift flow.
Buying stage: decision, with a hard deadline (holiday delivery).

### 2.3 Daily tea drinkers
People who already drink tea every day and want a steady supply with some variety. They care about value, freshness, and not being locked in. They need to know what a subscription costs per month and how to pause or skip.
Buying stage: decision.

**Where they spend time:** their inbox. That is the only channel this campaign uses, so the whole job is subject lines, send timing, and one clear next step.

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message:** The Steep Club is open. Pick a box, start your subscription, and your first box ships [first ship date].

Message hierarchy: why care, what it is, why us, what to do next.

**Supporting messages (confirm product facts before use):**

1. **For beginners:** "Start with [number] teas chosen for people new to loose-leaf, plus brewing notes for each one." Why it matters: removes the guesswork.
2. **For gift buyers:** "Give a subscription. They choose their teas; you choose how many months. Order by [holiday order-by date] for delivery before [date]." Why it matters: a gift that fits the person.
3. **For daily drinkers:** "A box every month: [number] teas, [weight] each, [price] per month. Pause or skip any month." Why it matters: steady supply, no lock-in.

**Proof points:** none are on file. Do not invent reviews, percentages, awards, or "thousands of happy drinkers." Use only confirmed product facts and real shipping dates. If the owner has real reviews later, they can be added with permission.

**Words to avoid:** best-in-class, AI-powered, game-changer, instant, detox, cleanse, and any word that promises more than the sentence can show. No guaranteed results. No revenue claims.

## 4. Channel Strategy

**Email only.** This is the studio constraint and the right fit: the audience is a waitlist, so email is the channel they expect.

| Element | Choice | Why | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| List and segments | One waitlist, split into beginners, gift buyers, daily drinkers | Different angle per group; avoids sending the same email seven times | Low |
| Send format | Short HTML with a plain-text version | Reads well on phones; plain text helps deliverability | Low |

## 7. Success Metrics

**Primary KPI:** 300 new paid subscriptions by 3 January 2027 (15% of 2,000).

**Secondary KPIs:**

| Metric | Why it matters | How to track |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate per send | Whether subject lines work | Email platform |
| Click-to-checkout rate | Whether the email earns the click | Email platform plus checkout analytics |
| Subscription rate per segment | Which group converts | Checkout source tag per link |
| Unsubscribe rate | Whether we are mailing too hard | Email platform |
| Refund or cancel rate in first 14 days | Whether the promise matched the product | Billing records |
| Gift vs self-purchase mix | Whether December timing worked | Checkout field |

**No invented benchmarks.** There is no past campaign data in this studio. Record Send 1 as the baseline, then compare each send and each segment to it.

**Checkpoints:**
- After each send: numbers at 24 hours and 72 hours.
- Sunday 20 December (end of week 2): if subscriptions are below 120, change week 3 and 4 rather than repeating week 1 and 2.
- Sunday 3 January: full report; decide whether to run a second campaign and to which segment.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

1. **15% is a high bar for email alone.** The list is a waitlist, which helps, but four weeks is short.
   Mitigation: segment the angles, resend to non-openers, and use the 20 December checkpoint to change course instead of repeating.
2. **The brand file does not describe a tea business.** Copy could drift or sound generic.
   Mitigation: follow the voice mechanics from `brand/voice.md`, confirm every product fact, and ban hype and health claims. Consider a short tea positioning note in `brand/` before copy is final.
3. **December shipping cuts the gift window short.** A late order-by date would break the gift promise.
   Mitigation: confirm the real cutoff first, send the gift angle in week 1, and state the cutoff plainly in the subject line and body.
4. **List age and deliverability.** A waitlist that has not been mailed in a while may land in spam.
   Mitigation: authenticate the domain, send from one consistent address, include plain-text versions, and keep unsubscribe one click.
5. **The studio rule against sending to real people.** An automated send would break it.
   Mitigation: the agent drafts only; a named person approves and sends; tests go to internal addresses.
